Pronunciation key

Every name carries a broad IPA transcription of how it is said in its origin language, as a speaker of the capital would render it. ˈ marks the stressed syllable, dots divide syllables, ː marks a long vowel. The plain respelling beside it is derived from the IPA (capitals for the stressed syllable).

Vowels: i see, ɪ sit, e/eː say without the glide, ɛ bed, æ cat, a/ɑ father, ɔ thought, o/oː go without the glide, ʊ put, u moon, ʌ cup, ə about, y French u or German ü (ee with rounded lips), ø/œ French eu or Norse ø (eh with rounded lips), ɑ̃ ɛ̃ ɔ̃ œ̃ the nasal vowels of French. Diphthongs: ai eye, au cow, ei day, oi boy, ou go, øy Norse øy (close to boy), eu Welsh eu (close to ay).

Consonants: j yes, w wet, ŋ sing, ɲ canyon, ʃ ship, ʒ measure, tʃ chip, dʒ jam, ts cats, dz adze, θ thin, ð this, x loch, ç German ich, ɬ Welsh ll (an h at the sides of the tongue), hr a breathy r, ʁ French r, r rolled r, ħ Arabic deep h, ʕ Arabic ʿayn, ʔ the catch in uh-oh, q a k far back in the throat, ɣ a soft g. In Chinese names: ɕ a lighter sh, tɕ a lighter ch, ɥ a y with rounded lips, ɨ a vowel between i and u.
